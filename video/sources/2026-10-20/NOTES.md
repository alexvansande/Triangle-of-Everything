## Fact check (rec21)

Right: the chart is a density scatter plot with size across and mass up; stars form
in nurseries, become protostars and live on the main sequence, then swell into red
giants; white dwarfs, neutron stars and black holes are the dense cores left
behind; all known black holes sit on the Schwarzschild line, the top-left limit
that nothing crosses; anything compact enough becomes a black hole; stellar black
holes are about 5 to 100 Suns (the lightest known are about 3–5); Sgr A* is about
4 million Suns; the biggest are less dense than water; M87* was the first
photographed (2019); TON 618, about 40–66 billion Suns (the chart uses 66), has a
horizon larger than the planets' orbits; in theory any mass can be squeezed into a
black hole; the early universe may have made them; quasar light (the Lyman-alpha
forest), the CMB, gravitational waves and microlensing each rule out a range of
masses, and Hawking radiation the lightest; what's left is about a small moon's
mass in a virus-sized hole down to an asteroid's in something smaller than an atom;
they'd be dark and felt only through gravity; galaxies and clusters weigh several to
ten times their visible matter; the window may close further with neutron-star
studies; galaxies sit on their own diagonal, mostly empty space; the largest
structures reach the average density of the universe, and voids are emptier still;
the line leads to the Hubble radius.

2. "black holes are typically the size of a star": a few Suns' mass, yes, but the

Wrong, still in the cut (worth a re-take if convenient):
4. "**All** of the black holes we know of are formed in the collapsing core of a

### Fact check (rec22)

Right, as in rec21, plus two improvements: the formation line now says "or are mergers
of other black holes", and the ending ("most of what we believe to be empty space …
filled with asteroids compressed to the size of an atom") is a fair "it's still possible".

Imprecise, worth knowing:
1. "formed in the collapsing core of a dying star **or are mergers** of other black
   holes": better than before; the supermassive ones grew mostly by swallowing gas, with
   mergers, from seeds nobody has pinned down. Fine as spoken.
2. "very old, **cold** primordial black holes": small black holes are the opposite of
   cold. Hawking radiation makes them hotter the smaller they are: an asteroid-mass one
   would be at roughly 10⁴–10⁹ K, though its glow is far too faint to see. "Very old,
   dark primordial black holes" would be exact.
3. "objects tiny as a virus with the mass of a small moon … asteroid compressed to the
   size of an atom": right; the asteroid-mass ones are a few hundred times smaller than an
   atom (the scene says "smaller than atoms").
4. "most of the mass in the universe is formed of something **very heavy** and
   invisible": most of the matter (about 85%) is dark matter; "heavy" isn't needed (it's
   a lot of mass, not a heavy substance).
5. "most of the matter has been compressed into tiny black holes … and we, everything
   else, are just the lucky survivors": a nice image, and right if primordial black holes
   were all of the dark matter (ordinary matter would then be the ~15% that didn't
   collapse); it's framed as a possibility, which it is.
6. Still not in this take: why the line is 45° (event horizon grows with mass).

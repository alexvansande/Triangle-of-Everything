A singularity — but not *the* singularity, and nothing to do with the singularities inside black holes. It is an object of about one Planck mass (~20 micrograms) squeezed down to a single Planck length. That puts it exactly at the apex of the triangle, where two limits meet: it is at once too small to be an ordinary particle (it sits on the Compton wavelength line) and too dense to escape becoming a black hole (it sits on the Schwarzschild radius line).

Those two boundaries only touch here, so an object like this could exist for just the briefest instant — a single Planck time at the very start of the Big Bang, when the whole universe was this hot and dense. In the original Lineweaver–Patel diagram it is called the *instanton*.

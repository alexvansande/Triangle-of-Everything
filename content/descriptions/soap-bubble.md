A soap bubble is a thin liquid shell surrounding air. Its shifting colours come from interference between light reflected from the film's two surfaces.

This **representative estimate** is a 3 cm bubble with an assumed 1 micrometre water-density film. The film weighs about 2.8 mg, while the enclosed air weighs about 17.3 mg at 15°C and sea-level pressure: **about 20 mg total**. Film thickness changes as the liquid drains, so this is not a universal bubble mass.

The plotted mass includes the air, not just the film. Its average density is slightly above that of the surrounding air.

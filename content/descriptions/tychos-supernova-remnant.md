Tycho’s supernova remnant: the shell left by the new star Tycho Brahe watched in 1572, bright enough to see by day, which convinced him the heavens could change. It was the other kind of supernova: not a collapsing giant but a white dwarf pushed past its limit, blown apart entirely, with no core left behind. The shell is about 7 parsecs across today and holds the dwarf's 1.4 Suns of ejecta plus the few Suns of gas it has swept up. The picture is Chandra's X-ray view (NASA/CXC/RIKEN and GSFC/T. Sato et al.; optical: DSS): the hot shocked gas in false colour.

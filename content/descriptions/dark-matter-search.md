Roughly 85% of the universe's mass is dark matter, yet its fundamental nature remains one of the deepest open questions in physics. Three major classes of candidates span vastly different regions of the Triangle:

**[[WIMP*|WIMPs]]** (Weakly Interacting Massive Particles) sit at the [[Electroweak]] scale ($10^{1}$–$10^{3}$ GeV), near the Compton line among the [[Fundamental Particles]]. They were long considered the leading candidates because the weak-scale annihilation cross section naturally yields the observed relic abundance (the "WIMP miracle"), but decades of direct detection experiments — including XENON, LUX, and PandaX — have found no confirmed signal, pushing limits deep into the predicted parameter space.

**MACHOs** (Massive Astrophysical Compact Halo Objects), such as brown dwarfs, dim stars and stellar remnants, sit near the stars on this chart. Microlensing surveys such as MACHO, EROS and OGLE have ruled them out as most of the dark matter.

**[[Primordial Black Hole|Primordial black holes]]** sit on the Schwarzschild line. Hawking radiation rules out the light ones and microlensing the heavy ones, which leaves a window of about $10^{17}$–$10^{22}$ g, marked on the line, where they could still be all of the dark matter.

**Macro dark matter** is any dark matter made of dense lumps much heavier than a particle, such as nuggets of quark matter. Ancient mica (which would carry their tracks), the cosmic microwave background, white dwarfs (which they would set off as supernovae) and microlensing have ruled out the fluffier ones. Objects between roughly nuclear density and the Schwarzschild line, over most masses, have not been ruled out, though few searches can reach them.

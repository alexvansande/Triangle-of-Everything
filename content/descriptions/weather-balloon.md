A weather balloon carries instruments upward as the surrounding atmosphere gets thinner. Falling pressure lets its lifting gas expand.

This **representative launch model** uses a Kaymont HAB-600's documented 600 g latex envelope, an assumed 500 g instrument-and-rigging load, and a 1.5 m spherical helium fill. Including about 300 g of helium gives **about 1.4 kg total**. Gas conditions are 15°C and sea-level pressure; size refers to the balloon, excluding the long suspension line.

The chart also includes **Weather Balloon (near burst)**: the same mass, expanded to about 6 m across. Neither point represents every weather-balloon launch.

[Kaymont HAB-600 specifications](https://www.kaymont.com/product-page/hab-600). The [National Weather Service](https://www.weather.gov/chs/upperair) describes launch balloons around five feet across.

Heating the air inside a balloon lowers its density, allowing the displaced cooler air to support the envelope, basket and passengers.

This is a **representative loaded model** using the Cameron A-77's published envelope volume of 77,500 cubic feet, about 2,195 m³, and envelope height of about 17.4 m. Assume 500 kg for the envelope, basket, burner, fuel and occupants, plus about 2,076 kg of enclosed air at 100°C and sea-level pressure: **about 2.58 tonnes total**.

That total includes the hot air itself; it is not the manufacturer's certified lifting weight. Size uses envelope height, excluding the basket and suspension. Operating temperature and load change from flight to flight.

[Cameron A-Type specifications](https://cameronballoons.com/a-type/). Gas mass uses the [ideal-gas relationship](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/equation-of-state/) and [standard atmospheric density](https://www-mdp.eng.cam.ac.uk/web/library/enginfo/aerothermal_dvd_only/aero/atmos/).

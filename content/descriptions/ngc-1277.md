NGC 1277 is a relic galaxy that stopped forming stars about 10 billion years ago, preserving a snapshot of the early universe. It contains one of the most massive black holes known relative to its size—the supermassive black hole weighs roughly 5 billion solar masses — early estimates ran as high as 17 billion — while the galaxy itself is relatively compact. This "fossil" galaxy challenges our understanding of how galaxies and their central black holes co-evolve.

**Uluru** (Ayers Rock), in Australia's Northern Territory, is a single mass of arkose sandstone **3.6 km long, 1.9 km wide and 348 m high**, sacred to the Anangu people. Its red comes from iron oxide rusting on the surface; inside, the rock is grey.

Its mass here is an estimate for the part above the plain only: about **1 km³** of rock at 2.6 g/cm³, so roughly **2.6 billion tonnes**. The rock carries on kilometres down into the ground, so "how heavy is Uluru" depends on where you stop counting, the same problem as weighing a mountain.

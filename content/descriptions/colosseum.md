The **Colosseum** (the Flavian Amphitheatre, Rome, finished in 80 AD) is an ellipse **189 m by 156 m** and **48 m** high, built for some 50,000 spectators. Its outer wall alone used over **100,000 m³ of travertine**, held together with about 300 t of iron clamps; the pockmarks on the facade are where those clamps were dug out in the Middle Ages.

Nobody has weighed it, so its mass here is an estimate: about **400,000 t** of travertine, tuff, brick and Roman concrete (published totals run from 300,000 to 750,000 t). Spread over the oval it encloses, it lands well below the water line: like the Eiffel Tower, most of what it "fills" is the arena and the air inside.

Everything we can observe — plotted here at the Hubble radius, a sphere with a radius of ~14 billion light-years containing all known matter (~10⁵⁶ g). Remarkably, that sphere sits almost exactly on its own Schwarzschild radius: the universe is as dense as a black hole its size. (Expansion has since carried the most distant sources we can see out to ~46.5 billion light-years — the origin of the often-quoted "93 billion light-years across.")

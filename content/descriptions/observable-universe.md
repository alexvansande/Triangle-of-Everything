Everything we can see. The oldest light, the cosmic microwave background, left its source 13.8 billion years ago, and that source is now about 46 billion light-years away: the origin of the often-quoted "93 billion light-years across." It lands outside the triangle because it isn't one object: its far sides have never been able to reach each other, and most of its galaxies are already beyond our cosmic event horizon, out of reach even at the speed of light. What we see is the light they sent long ago, when they were much closer. So it is less a view of where our neighbours are, and more a history of our own past. The mass plotted is everything inside that sphere at the average density of the universe, about 3 × 10⁵⁷ g.

**PSR J0030+0451**, a pulsar about 1,100 light-years away spinning 205 times a second, was the first neutron star whose size was measured: NICER timed the hot spots on its surface rotating in and out of view and found about 1.4 Suns packed into a ball some 25 km across.

The collapsed core of a massive star after a supernova explosion. Composed almost entirely of neutrons, with densities exceeding that of atomic nuclei — a sugar-cube-sized amount would weigh about a billion tonnes. Neutron stars can spin hundreds of times per second and have magnetic fields a trillion times stronger than [[Earth]]'s.

---
title: Hubble Radius
---

The distance at which space is moving away from us at the speed of light: about 14.4 billion light-years (1.37 × 10²⁸ cm). The farther away something is, the faster it recedes, and here that speed reaches the speed of light.

It is not a horizon. The Hubble sphere itself is growing, so light sent today from a little beyond it will still reach us. The true edge, the cosmic event horizon, is a little further out.

A sphere of this radius, filled at the average density of the universe, sits exactly on the black-hole line.
